Audio sources & licences

Mehtronome combines recorded drum and instrument samples with modeled instruments and sustained harmony. Sources for the Original and Expanded libraries are listed below.

Piano and guitars

Recordings from Tone.js instruments, edited by Nicolas Brosowsky, under CC BY 3.0. Original source credits: piano — Versilian Studios / VSCO 2; electric guitar — Karoryfer Samples; nylon guitar — quartertone, classical guitar multisamples.

Upright bass

Meatbass, Karoryfer Samples / Drogomir Smolken, recorded by Ludwik Zamenhof. A 1958 Otto Rubner double bass, using soft single-voice pizzicato plucks, under CC0 1.0. Mehtronome tunes the recordings and gently filters upper overtones offline.

Latin percussion

Versilian Community Sample Library, Sam Gossner / Versilian Studios and contributors, under CC0 1.0. Acoustic claves, open and muted congas, tumba, cowbell, güiro and shaker. Trimmed, gently filtered, downmixed and converted to mono 32 kHz WAV.

Tambourine

VSCO 2 Community Edition, Versilian Studios / Sam Gossner, under CC0 1.0. VSCO 1 percussion tambourine recordings.

Club jazz drums

Virtuosity Drums, Versilian Studios with Karoryfer Samples, performed by Austin McMahon at Virtuosity Musical Instruments, Boston. Released under CC0 1.0. Selected soft and medium hits, cross-sticks, toms and multiple cymbal takes. Used for jazz, Latin, bossa, soul and ballad styles.

Live fusion drums

DRSKit, by Lars Muldjord, Bent Bisballe Nyeng and Jes Eiler / DRSDrums; SFZ/FLAC port by kinwie. Released under CC BY 4.0. Selected close and overhead microphones, gentle filtering, trimmed silence, normalization and edge fades. Original tuning retained; kick tails are shortened to reduce boom while the other acoustic decays remain. Used for pop/rock, blues, fusion jazz, Motown, funk, second-line, reggae, boogaloo, hard rock and legato fusion.

Earlier hybrid drums

Big Rusty Drums, Karoryfer Samples, under CC0 1.0. Recorded close hits are reshaped into a modern hybrid kit: short envelopes, focused EQ, higher snare tuning, soft saturation and original kick/snare body reinforcement. Retained in the source for reproducibility; replaced in playback by the live acoustic bank. These are edited derivatives, not a new recording session.

Legacy acoustic drums

Wilkinson Audio Naked Drums, Wilkinson Audio, under CC BY 4.0.

Electric bass

Black And Blue Basses, Karoryfer Samples / D. Smolken, under CC0 1.0.

Changes for this app

Selected recordings were downmixed, trimmed, filtered, peak-normalized and faded, then converted to mono 32 kHz 16-bit WAV. Playback corrects measured pitch centres. Pop/rock, blues, fusion jazz, Motown, funk, second-line, reggae, boogaloo, hard rock and legato fusion use the DRSKit live acoustic bank; the lighter styles use the separate Virtuosity bank. Motown adds recorded tambourine. Blues guitar adds mild drive. Hard rock uses the same electric-guitar recordings with stronger drive, filtered separately for each voice. Legato fusion softens the recorded guitar attack for chord swells; its connected flute-like melodic voice is synthesized. Mellow jazz guitar uses the existing electric-guitar recordings with a darker filter. Soft piano uses the same piano recordings with softer filtering.

Expanded sound library

Expanded recordings adds 126 recordings while preserving styles, arrangements, mixer and FX. These are edited sample-library recordings, not newly recorded sessions.

  • Black And Green Guitars, Karoryfer Samples / D. Smolken, CC0: soft and medium sustained electric-guitar recordings, two independent takes per pitch and separate recorded staccato articulations.
  • Additional soft and strong Black And Blue Basses plucks, Karoryfer Samples / D. Smolken, CC0. Together with the original medium-soft plucks these supply three recorded attack levels. Additional soft Meatbass takes retain its existing warm fundamental treatment.
  • Additional piano and nylon-guitar pitches and an acoustic flute from Tone.js instruments, Nicolas Brosowsky, CC BY 3.0; original piano and guitar source credits as above. The recorded flute replaces the modeled flute-like voice only in Expanded recordings.
  • Greg Sullivan’s E-Pianos, Greg Sullivan, mapped by kinwie, CC BY 3.0: Wurlitzer EP200 soft and strong layers. Expanded electric-key patches use these reed-piano recordings; they are not Rhodes recordings.
  • VCSL, Sam Gossner / Versilian Studios and contributors, CC0: velocity-layered Yamaha TX81Z Clavisynth recordings. This is a sampled clav-style synthesizer, not an acoustic Clavinet.
  • Additional soft DRSKit closed-hat and ride takes, same attribution and CC BY 4.0 licence as above. Existing kick treatment, kit assignments and cymbal balance are retained.

Derivatives are downmixed, onset-trimmed, gently filtered, peak-normalized, edge-faded and converted to mono 32 kHz 16-bit PCM WAV. Playback uses measured fundamentals or documented source tuning. Source revisions, hashes, transformations and full licences are retained with the source in samples/expanded. Expanded organ remains synthesized, with nine drawbar partials, percussion and rotary-style amplitude modulation. Connected modeled guitar leads and sustained harmony remain synthesized.

Selectable acoustic drum kits and wider dynamics

MuldjordKit, Lars Muldjord / DrumGizmo; SFZ/FLAC port by kinwie, CC BY 4.0. A Tama Superstar acoustic kit recorded in 2010. Forty selected recordings supply multiple kick, snare, closed-hat, ride and tom strengths, plus open hat, bell and crash. Existing credited DRS pedal hats and Virtuosity cross-sticks complete this kit. Kicks are gently filtered and shortened; original tuning is retained. Other hits use gentle filtering, onset trim, peak normalization and edge fades, converted to mono 32 kHz 16-bit WAV.

Eighteen additional DRSKit recordings expand kick, snare, hats, toms, ride-bell and crash dynamics under the same DRS attribution and CC BY 4.0 licence above. Sources, source/derivative hashes and full licences are retained in samples/tama. The standard palette uses the appropriate Tama, DRS and Virtuosity recording sessions by style. Naked Drums remains in the source for reproducibility.
