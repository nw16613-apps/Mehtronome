# Modern straight-eighth jazz and upright definition

Modern Straight-Eighth Jazz now uses a four-bar half-time groove: main snare on beat three, displaced kick/ghost-note answers, broken straight ride phrases and hi-hat foot on two and four. Short tom answers finish longer phrases. Automatic arrangements, reference studies and fallback ensembles use this vocabulary. Fusion Jazz retains its separate hi-hat/backbeat groove; Legato Fusion is unchanged.

Upright bass keeps the existing Sneakybass recordings. No replacement library is claimed. Its processing now preserves more of the recorded pluck: maximum 2.5 ms attack, less low-pass darkening, and a modest broad low-body reduction around 220 Hz. Pitch, note gates, sample choice, channel gain and electric-bass processing remain unchanged. This applies wherever the upright instrument is used.

Checks cover the four-bar pulse, straight grid, arrangement/ensemble use, real-bank mix headroom, bass pitch/gate/gain retention, initial pluck energy relative to low-body energy, release to silence, and browser style selection. Listening feedback remains the judge of the preferred sound.
