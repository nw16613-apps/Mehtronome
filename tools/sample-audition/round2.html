Recording & amplification audition · round two

Guitars through an amp.
Cleaner bass candidates.

New: subtle chorus for your preferred Emilyguitar. In the first comparison, switch between Emilyguitar · amp and Emilyguitar · amp + chorus. The latter adds slow stereo modulation after the amp/cabinet, with an undelayed dry attack. Headphones will make the width easier to hear.

Emilyguitar and Yamaha RBX are selected initially to reflect your preferences. Compare any version against Current, your existing app sound. All versions play the same notes, accents and timing. Start with Solo, then listen In the band.

Guitar: Emilyguitar is a different Epiphone recording source. Fender DI is the unprocessed version of the current Fender source—a control for hearing the new amp treatment. Compare each DI against its amp version.
Bass: Yamaha RBX finger recordings and flatwound Swagbass replace Growlybass in this test. Listen especially to the last half of the Funk clip.

These are listening candidates. The main app’s instruments and playback have not changed.

Chorus: the clean Emily amp version has a second option with 0.32 Hz modulation, a 14 ms centre delay, 1.3 ms depth and 18% wet signal. Left/right modulation is offset for subtle width; the wet signal is filtered to leave the dry body and attack clear. No feedback or long echoes are added. The existing app has no clean-guitar chorus stage; FreePats documents baked-in amp/effects processing but does not identify a chorus, so its presence in the original recording is unconfirmed.

The amp chain: input/coupling filters → two soft-clipping stages with envelope-dependent sag → tone filtering → recorded speaker response. Saturation runs at 4× sample rate before downsampling. Clean/Fusion settings use light drive; Blues uses more breakup. This is an original lightweight model, not a circuit-accurate or neural capture of a named amplifier. The cabinet response is a public-domain Fender Deluxe Reverb IR from the source below.

Current has its original baked-in amp processing and is not processed through the new amp again. Candidate DI versions bypass the old per-note distortion and retain the sampler’s note envelope. A cabinet IR supplies speaker/microphone coloration; it does not create hammer-ons, pull-offs or palm mutes. Those articulations are not newly implemented in this audition.

All solo versions are delivered in stereo and matched in integrated loudness using fixed gain, without a limiter. In the band, only the changed instrument receives a source-level correction; every other stem stays identical. Each complete mix receives the same fixed gain within its comparison. Main-app reverb/delay returns are omitted from these ensemble renders.

Availability: SampleScience could not be downloaded because its hosts are blocked in this environment. Standard Guitar’s publisher terms prohibit altering or redistributing its sample data without permission, so it is not bundled here. Yamaha RBX has a sparse one-octave recording map; higher notes are transposed, which may affect your preference. No sample is claimed to be buzz-free before listening.
